## Timeline metafields (`custom.brand_timeline` → brand_timeline_entry)

### 1. 1830–1862 · A hatter's son goes west

**era:** `1830–1862`  
**title:** `A hatter's son goes west`  
**body:**

```
John Batterson Stetson was born in East Orange, New Jersey in 1830 — the
seventh of twelve children — and learned the trade at his father's bench.
Tuberculosis sent him west. He rose in a St. Joseph, Missouri brickyard
until a river flood wiped the factory out, then joined a Pike's Peak gold
party in 1862. In a Colorado rainstorm he felted beaver fur the way his
father taught him and made a tall, wide-brimmed hat. A passing bullwhacker
bought it for a five-dollar gold piece. First Stetson ever sold.
```

### 2. 1865 · A hat with no crease

**era:** `1865`  
**title:** `A hat with no crease`  
**body:**

```
Healthy again, Stetson went back to Philadelphia with sixty dollars borrowed
from his sister. On January 17, 1865 — Benjamin Franklin's birthday — he
opened a one-room shop at 7th and Callowhill and cut the Boss of the Plains:
smooth dome crown, flat brim, no dents, no decoration. It shipped blank on
purpose. The man who bought it put in his own crease, and no two ended up
alike.
```

### 3. 1870–1900 · The largest hat factory on earth

**era:** `1870–1900`  
**title:** `The largest hat factory on earth`  
**body:**

```
The Boss sold faster than one room could make it. By 1870 Stetson had moved
into a three-story building a block from Independence Hall. He sent a Boss
to every Western dealer with a letter asking for a dozen more. By the turn
of the century he ran the biggest hat works in the world — thousands of
hands, a hospital and lending library on the Philadelphia campus, and a
grand prize at the 1900 Paris Exposition. The name had stopped meaning a
company and started meaning the hat itself.
```

### 4. 1922–1923 · The last drop

**era:** `1922–1923`  
**title:** `The last drop`  
**body:**

```
Arizona cowboy turned painter Lon Megargee painted a rider tipping his hat
brim full of water to his horse. Stetson bought the painting and the rights
in 1923 and ran it in their advertising the year after. Look inside a
Stetson today and it is still printed there.
```

### 5. 1920s–1940s · Hollywood picks a hat

**era:** `1920s–1940s`  
**title:** `Hollywood picks a hat`  
**body:**

```
Tom Mix made nearly 300 films and wore a Stetson through most of them. The
company built his hats to order and named a crown after him — Gus and Tom
Mix are still on our shape list. Gene Autry, Roy Rogers, and John Wayne
followed, and the western hat became something you recognized before the
actor.
```

### 6. Today · Shaped in Fayetteville

**era:** `Today`  
**title:** `Shaped in Fayetteville`  
**body:**

```
Stetsons are made in Garland, Texas now, at the only vertically integrated
fur felt mill in North America — same roof that turns out Resistol and
Charlie 1 Horse. Most arrive already creased: cattleman, pinch front,
whatever the line was built for. Good felt doesn't mind. It takes steam and
it takes a new shape, so the crease a hat left the factory with isn't the
one you're stuck with. We set it to your head at the bench, two doors south
of the bowling alley.
```
