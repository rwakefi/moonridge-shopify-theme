The brim is shade, attitude, and balance. Same crown, different brim set — and the hat either disappears into the outfit or becomes the whole point.

These are the brim shapes we use on Moon Ridge product pages. Width still matters (most western brims land around three and a half to four and a half inches), but the curl and profile are what people notice across a room.

Flat / Pencil Curl

A flat brim with a tight pencil roll on the edge — clean, modern, a little fashion-forward.

Shows a lot of brim surface. Great with a strong crown when you want a sharper, contemporary western look. Needs a confident wear; there’s nowhere for a wavy edge to hide.

Snap Brim / Flanged Brim

A brim you can tip or snap — more city/fedora language than arena, but it shows up on western-adjacent and dress hats we carry.

Think versatility: brim down for weather and focus, brim up when you want open face and light.

RD (Round)

An even, rounded brim without a hard side-kick. Soft oval from every angle.

Friendly on longer faces and anyone who finds aggressive side curls too costume. Quiet confidence.

J (George Strait, Medium Curved)

The medium side curl associated with the George Strait look — classic country, not extreme, wearable to church and the sale barn.

If someone says “just a normal cowboy brim,” they usually mean something in J territory. Our most requested everyday set.

JB (Bullrider)

A stronger, more aggressive curl — bullrider energy. Sides come up with more intention; the front stays useful for sun.

Reads rodeo and performance. If J feels too soft and you want the hat to frame your face harder, JB is the next stop.

CHL (Cool Hand Luke, Shovel, Reiner Low Sides)

A shovel-influenced brim with lower sides in the Cool Hand Luke / reiner family. Distinct profile — once you know it, you spot it on the street.

Pairs naturally with a CHL crown, but we can mix. Ask us to set CHL brim on a cattleman if you want that hybrid.

U (Reiner High Sides)

Higher side curls in the reiner family — more lift along the edges, a bolder horseshoe of brim when you look straight on.

Lots of presence. Works when the crown can carry the height; can overwhelm a very short crown or a petite frame if overdone.

WTP (West Texas Punch, Rancher)

West Texas Punch / rancher brim — purposeful front and sides with that big-country utility look.

Built to look like it works for a living. Excellent with Texas Punch and cattleman crowns.

SC (Showmanship)

Showmanship brim — cleaner, presentation-minded set for the people who care how the hat photographs under lights and in the pen.

If you show, ask for SC language by name. We’ll match it to the crown your event actually wants.

Width, face, and shade

Wider brims add shade and can shorten a long face. Narrower brims feel dressier and suit smaller frames.

Higher side curls frame the face and show more cheekbone. Flatter brims feel modern and a little tougher to wear well — which is why we steam them carefully.

Can we change the brim?

On quality felt — and many straws — yes. Steam, blocks, and a patient hand move a brim from flat toward J, or ease a harsh curl that fights your face.

Complimentary shaping is for in-store purchases at Moon Ridge in Fayetteville. Hats bought elsewhere are $35 to shape/clean. Shipped online orders are not included in free shaping — come see us if you want the full brim set on your head.

Put the brim on last — then look again

We often lock the crown first, then dial the brim while you watch the mirror. That order keeps you from falling in love with a curl that doesn’t belong on your crease.
