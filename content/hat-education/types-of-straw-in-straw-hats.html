Not every “straw hat” is the same straw. Bangora, Shantung, palm leaf, and Panama each feel different in the hand, breathe differently in July heat, and sit in different price lanes.

Here’s the practical breakdown we use on the floor at Moon Ridge — where it comes from, what quality looks like, and what you typically pay. Price ranges below reflect what we usually see on western and dress straws in our shop (roughly mid‑2026); rare Montecristi Panamas and fashion outliers can run higher.

Bangora straw

Where it comes from: Bangora (often tied to Bangkok / Toyo-style paper straw) is a machine-friendly woven fiber used heavily in western straw cowboy hats. It’s engineered for consistency more than heirloom handwork.

Quality tells: A more open, vented look is common — especially in the crown. It feels a bit coarser than fine Shantung, which is exactly why a lot of working and everyday summer hats use it. Air moves. The hat stays light. It holds a western crease without asking you to baby it every weekend.

Best for: Daily summer wear, rodeos, ranch work, first straw cowboy hat.

Typical price range: About $40–$80 for solid branded Bangora westerns. On our wall right now you’ll see pieces like the Stetson White Horse and Stetson Billy Junior in that entry lane.

Shantung straw

Where it comes from: Shantung is a refined paper-fiber straw traditionally associated with East Asian production (the name tracks to China’s Shandong region). It’s one of the most common materials in mid-to-upper western and dress straw lines from Stetson, Resistol, and similar makers.

Quality tells: Smoother hand than Bangora. Tighter, more even weave. Takes dye and finish cleanly, so you get that polished “dress straw” look. Finer Shantung feels closer to a dress hat; coarser grades still work hard in the sun. When brands stamp an X-rating on straw (you’ll see 5X, 6X, 10X on some Open Roads and westerns), they’re usually signaling weave/finish grade within their own Shantung ladder — not the same scale as fur-felt X.

Best for: Summer hats you still want to look finished — Open Road styles, nicer western straws, travel.

Typical price range: About $70–$200+. In our shop that runs from everyday pieces like the Stetson Brentwood and Stetson Redwood up through dressier options like the Stetson Oasis, Stetson Primo, and Stetson Atacama.

Palm leaf straw

Where it comes from: Real palm leaf — dried and woven from palm fronds. This is plant fiber with a rustic character, not a paper-straw substitute.

Quality tells: Heavier hand than Bangora or fine Shantung. Visible leaf texture. Tough. Palm leaf shrugs off a hard day better than a delicate dress weave, and a lot of wearers like that it can take a little water and keep working. It won’t look as silky as a fine Panama — and it’s not trying to.

Best for: Work, fishing, beach-to-barn summers, anyone who beats up a hat.

Typical price range: About $55–$120 for branded western palm leaf. Examples on our floor: Stetson Lakeland, Stetson Chambers, Stetson Cumberland.

Panama straw (toquilla)

Where it comes from: True Panama hats are woven in Ecuador from toquilla palm fiber (Carludovica palmata) — not in Panama. The name stuck from history and shipping routes. Ecuadorian toquilla weaving is recognized by UNESCO for a reason: the finest weaves are slow, skilled handwork.

Quality tells: Look for Ecuador / toquilla language, not just the word “Panama” on a tag. Finer weave = lighter feel, smoother surface, better packability, and a higher price. Montecristi and Cuenca are the classic weaving centers; “superfino” territory is a different world from a casual tourist Panama. Breathability and longevity at the top end are excellent when you store and handle the hat with respect.

Honest caveat: Plenty of hats get marketed as “Panama” when they’re actually Shantung or Toyo. If the price is bargain-bin and there’s no Ecuador story, believe the weave, not the buzzword.

Best for: Dress summer wear, travel, safari/outback shapes, anyone who wants the real fiber story.

Typical price range: About $130–$250 for the genuine branded Panamas we carry (and much higher for museum-grade Montecristi elsewhere). Shop examples: Stetson The Naturalist, Stetson Airway, Stetson Arapaho, Stetson Center Dent Panama. Also see our Fedoras / Panamas collection.

Quick comparison

Bangora — vented workhorse · machine-consistent · ~$40–$80

Shantung — smoother dress/western staple · broad quality ladder · ~$70–$200+

Palm leaf — rustic natural fiber · tough in the weather · ~$55–$120

Panama (toquilla) — Ecuador hand-weave tradition · finest when the weave is tight · ~$130–$250+ (and up for elite grades)

How to choose in under a minute

Buy Bangora if you want cool air and a hardworking summer cowboy hat without spending heirloom money.

Buy Shantung if you want a cleaner, dressier straw that still lives in western shapes.

Buy palm leaf if your hat sees dirt, water spray, and long days outside.

Buy Panama if you want true toquilla fiber and a finer summer dress hat — and you’re willing to pay for the weave.
